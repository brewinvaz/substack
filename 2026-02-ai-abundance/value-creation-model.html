● The Complement Effect

When one input in a multiplicative system becomes cheap, the others become more valuable. As AI commoditizes general-purpose analytical labor, proprietary data, judgment, and execution speed become the limiting factors that determine competitive advantage.

Intelligence (Commoditizing)

Raw analytical horsepower is becoming cheap. Summarization, code generation, analysis, and pattern recognition are increasingly available via API. The cost of "have a smart person look at this" is collapsing across industries.

Proprietary Data (New Scarcity)

Data that can't be scraped from the internet. The context from your own operations, customer interactions, and deployment feedback. This becomes the training signal that makes your systems harder to replicate.

Judgment (New Scarcity)

Knowing what's worth doing. Problem selection, evaluation against ground truth, and the ability to recognize when AI outputs are "plausible but wrong." AI makes you faster at climbing hills; it doesn't tell you which hill to climb.

Execution Speed (New Scarcity)

How fast you close the loop between insight and action. The ability to deploy, measure, learn, and iterate. Speed compounds when combined with feedback loops that improve your systems with each cycle.
